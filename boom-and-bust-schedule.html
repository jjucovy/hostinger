Course Schedule & Readings

The course meets four times per week for 20 weeks. Readings are due on the first class meeting of the week shown. Political economists are assigned when their ideas are most directly illuminated by the historical events under study.

Key

P Primary source   H Secondary / historiographic   A Peer-reviewed article

WeekTopicReadings & Assignments
1What Is a Financial Crisis? The Dutch Golden Age H Kindleberger & Aliber, Manias, Panics, and Crashes, excerpts
Discussion: establishing a taxonomy of financial crises
2Tulipmania (1634–37) P Mackay, Extraordinary Popular Delusions (1841, excerpts) — Tulipomania
A Garber, “Tulipmania,” JEP
3The Mississippi Scheme and the South Sea Bubble (1719–20) P Mackay, Extraordinary Popular Delusions (1841, excerpts) — Mississippi Scheme & South Sea Bubble
P Defoe, Daniel. The Anatomy of Exchange-Alley (1719, excerpts)
A Harris, Ron. “The Bubble Act: Its Passage and Its Effects.” Journal of Economic History 54.3 (1994): 610–627.
Document Analysis Paper #1 Due  |  Research Paper Proposal Due
4Adam Smith and the Logic of Markets P Smith, Adam. Wealth of Nations (1776, excerpts)
H Heilbroner, The Worldly Philosophers, excerpts
Discussion: does Smith's invisible hand explain or preclude financial crisis?
5Say's Law: Can Markets Self-Correct? P Say, Jean-Baptiste. A Treatise on Political Economy (1803, excerpts) — “Of the Demand or Market for Products”
H Kindleberger & Aliber, Manias, Panics, and Crashes, excerpts
Discussion: if supply creates its own demand, what causes a depression?
6Railroads, Reconstruction, and the Panic of 1873 P Marx, Karl. Capital, Vol. I (1867, excerpts) — commodity form, M-C-M′ circuit
P Congressional testimony on the collapse of Jay Cooke & Co.
H Foner, Eric. Reconstruction, excerpts
A Mixon, Scott. “The Crisis of 1873: Perspectives from Multiple Asset Classes.” Journal of Economic History 68.3 (2008): 722–757.
Note: Marx assigned here to illuminate railroad speculation as capital accumulation.
7The Gilded Age: Conspicuous Wealth and the Silver Debate P Veblen, Thorstein. The Theory of the Leisure Class (1899, excerpts) — conspicuous consumption
P Bryan, William Jennings. “Cross of Gold” Speech (1896)
H Goodwyn, Lawrence. Democratic Promise, Introduction
A Rockoff, Hugh. “Some Evidence on the Real Price of Gold.” A Retrospective on the Classical Gold Standard. NBER/U Chicago, 1984 (course packet).
8Panics, the Lender of Last Resort, and the Birth of the Fed P Bagehot, Walter. Lombard Street (1873, excerpts) — the lender-of-last-resort doctrine
P Pujo Committee Report (1913, excerpts, course packet)
H Meltzer, Allan. A History of the Federal Reserve, Vol. I, excerpts
A Calomiris, Charles W. & Gary Gorton. “The Origins of Banking Panics.” Financial Markets and Financial Crises. U of Chicago, 1991 (course packet).
Annotated Bibliography Due
9The 1920s Boom and the Great Crash of 1929 P New York Times, Oct. 24–29, 1929 — front pages (course packet)
H Galbraith, The Great Crash 1929, excerpts
A White, Eugene N. “The Stock Market Boom and Crash of 1929 Revisited.” Journal of Economic Perspectives 4.2 (1990): 67–83.
10Causes of the Great Depression: The Historiographic Debate H Friedman & Schwartz, A Monetary History of the U.S., excerpts
H Eichengreen, Golden Fetters, excerpts
A Bernanke, Ben S. “Nonmonetary Effects of the Financial Crisis in the Propagation of the Great Depression.” American Economic Review 73.3 (1983): 257–276.
Structured debate: monetary vs. structural explanations for the Depression
Midterm Examination
11The New Deal: Keynes, Hayek, and the Battle Over Recovery P Roosevelt, FDR. First Inaugural Address (1933); Fireside Chat on Banking (March 12, 1933)
P Keynes, General Theory (1936, excerpts); Hayek, Prices and Production (1931, excerpts)
A Skidelsky, Robert. “The Relevance of Keynes.” Cambridge Journal of Economics 35.1 (2011): 1–13.
Note: Keynes and Hayek assigned here as direct responses to the Depression they lived through.
Document Analysis Paper #2 Due
12Designing the Post-War Order: Bretton Woods and Polanyi P Polanyi, Karl. The Great Transformation (1944, excerpts) — the self-regulating market and its discontents
P Keynes, Proposals for an International Clearing Union (excerpts); White, Proposal for an International Stabilization Fund (excerpts)
H Steil, Benn. The Battle of Bretton Woods, excerpts
Note: Polanyi's argument that unregulated markets destroy social fabric appears here as the intellectual context for Bretton Woods institution-building.
13Bretton Woods Simulation Active participation in the multi-nation Bretton Woods simulation.
Nations: USA, UK, France, West Germany, India, Argentina, USSR.
Students manage monetary policy and trade across the postwar recovery (1946–1958).
Simulation Reflection Paper assigned.
14The Long Boom, Stagflation, and the Collapse of Bretton Woods P Nixon, Richard. Address to the Nation on Economic Policy (August 15, 1971)
H Eichengreen, Barry. Globalizing Capital, excerpts
A Obstfeld, Maurice, Jay C. Shambaugh & Alan M. Taylor. “The Trilemma in History.” Review of Economics and Statistics 87.3 (2005): 423–438.
Simulation Reflection Paper Due
15Monetarism, Volcker, and the S&L Crisis P Weber, Max. The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism (1905, excerpts)
P Volcker, Paul. Statement before the Joint Economic Committee (1981)
H Meltzer, Allan. A History of the Federal Reserve, Vol. II, excerpts
A Curry, Timothy & Lynn Shibut. “The Cost of the Savings and Loan Crisis.” FDIC Banking Review 13.2 (2000): 26–35.
Note: Weber assigned alongside Volcker — both invoke moral discipline as the antidote to inflationary excess.
16Junk Bonds, Black Monday, and the 1980s Boom P Brady Commission Report on the Market Break (1988) — executive summary
A Jensen, Michael C. “Eclipse of the Public Corporation.” Harvard Business Review 67.5 (1989): 61–74.
A Roll, Richard. “The International Crash of October 1987.” Financial Analysts Journal 44.5 (1988): 19–35.
17The Asian Crisis, LTCM, and the Dot-Com Bust P Greenspan, Alan. “Irrational Exuberance” Speech (1996)
H Shiller, Robert. Irrational Exuberance, excerpts
A Radelet, Steven & Jeffrey Sachs. “The East Asian Financial Crisis.” Brookings Papers on Economic Activity 1 (1998): 1–74.
Document Analysis Paper #3 Due  |  Research Paper Draft Due
18The Housing Bubble and the Crash of 2008 P Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission Report (2011), excerpts
H Lewis, Michael. The Big Short, excerpts
A Brunnermeier, Markus K. “Deciphering the Liquidity and Credit Crunch 2007–2008.” Journal of Economic Perspectives 23.1 (2009): 77–100.
Oral presentations begin
19After the Crash: Minsky, Dodd-Frank, and the Politics of Recovery P Minsky, Hyman. Stabilizing an Unstable Economy (1986, excerpts) — financial instability hypothesis
H Blinder, Alan. After the Music Stopped, excerpts
A Funke, Manuel, Moritz Schularick & Christoph Trebesch. “Going to Extremes: Politics after Financial Crises.” European Economic Review 88 (2016): 227–260.
Note: Minsky assigned here because the 2008 crisis produced his rediscovery — read him as a retrospective diagnosis.
Oral presentations continue
19Patterns Across Crises: What Do We Actually Learn? H Reinhart & Rogoff, This Time Is Different, excerpts — conclusions
H Kindleberger & Aliber, Manias, Panics, and Crashes, excerpts — comparative patterns
A Laeven, Luc & Fabian Valencia. “Systemic Banking Crises Database: An Update.” IMF Working Paper WP/12/163 (2012) — summary.
Socratic Seminar: what patterns hold? What lessons actually stick?
20Course Synthesis and Final Examination Cumulative review across all 20 weeks.
Research Papers Due
Socratic Seminar: “What is the single most important lesson of economic history?”
Final Examination — document analysis (30%), comparative essay (40%), short identifications (30%)
