Readings & Assignments by Unit

Each unit pairs primary-source, historiographic, and textbook readings with a written or analytical assignment. Full source packets are distributed at the start of each unit; page-level assignments are posted to the class site.

UnitReadingsAssignment
1
The World Before Progress: Stability-Seeking Societies
  • Excerpt: Robert Nisbet, History of the Idea of Progress, ch. 1 (historiographic — traces progress as a belief with a specific origin, not a historical given)
  • Selections from a medieval guild charter (primary source)
  • Excerpts from a medieval communal charter granted to a religious minority (primary source)
 Source analysis: compare two charters (guild and minority community) and identify the stability mechanisms each encodes. In a short reflection, note what assumptions a “progress” narrative would have to overlook to call this era simply “backward.”
2
New Empires, Old Worlds (1400–1600)
  • Primary source: the 1492 Edict of Expulsion (excerpted)
  • Excerpt: Petrarch, letters articulating the “Dark Ages” — the original decline-and-light narrative that later gets inverted into a story of progress
  • Excerpt: Giorgio Vasari, Lives of the Artists, preface (primary source — an early narrative of artistic “progress” from Cimabue to Michelangelo)
  • Excerpts from Machiavelli, The Prince
  • Excerpts from Bodin
 Short essay: how did the Spanish state use religious policy to build a unified, stable realm? In a short reflection, trace the line from Petrarch's “Dark Ages” (a story of decline from a lost golden age) to Vasari's story of artistic “progress” (a story of ascent) — what does each narrative assume, and what does the course ask students to question in both?
3
Reformation, Absolutism, and Religious War (1600–1700)
  • Primary source: excerpts from the Peace of Westphalia (1648)
  • Excerpts from Descartes
  • Excerpts from Reuchlin
  • Primary source: an Ottoman millet decree
  • Eyewitness account excerpt describing the Khmelnytsky Uprising
  • Historiographic excerpt: interpretations of absolutism as stability vs. tyranny
 Document-based question: was absolutism a rational response to the instability of religious war? Support with at least three sources.
4
The Scientific Revolution and the Enlightenment (1700–1789)
  • Primary source: excerpt from a philosophe (e.g., Condorcet) on progress and reason
  • Primary source: a Haskalah maskil's essay on education reform (excerpted)
  • Historiographic excerpt: Peter Gay or comparable historian on the Enlightenment's origins
  • Textbook: “Science, Reason, and the Enlightened State” survey chapter
 Reading response: identify one Enlightenment idea and trace how a minority reform movement adapted it.
5
Revolution and Empire (1789–1815)
  • Primary source: Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen (1789)
  • Primary source: National Assembly debate excerpt on minority citizenship
  • Primary source: excerpt from the Congress of Vienna settlement
  • Historiographic excerpt: debate over the meaning of “emancipation” as a bargain
 Essay: was revolutionary-era emancipation a genuine expansion of rights, or a conditional bargain? Use at least two primary sources.
6
Industrialization, Nationalism, and Reform (1815–1880)
  • Primary source: factory inspector's report or worker testimony (industrial England)
  • Primary source: excerpt from an Ottoman Tanzimat reform decree
  • Historiographic excerpt: comparing top-down and bottom-up models of 19th-century reform
  • Textbook: “Industrialization and Nationalism” survey chapter
 Comparative chart and short response: Tanzimat reform vs. Italian/German unification as two paths to modern statehood.
7
Imperialism, Race, and the Crisis of Progress (1880–1914)
  • Primary source: newspaper coverage excerpt of the Dreyfus Affair
  • Primary source: excerpt from a nationalist manifesto (Balkan, Zionist, or Arab)
  • Historiographic excerpt: critique of “scientific racism” in the 19th century
  • Textbook: “The Age of Empire” survey chapter
 Source analysis: what does the Dreyfus Affair reveal about the strength and limits of liberal institutions circa 1900?
8
War and Revolution: The Unraveling of the Old Order (1914–1939)
  • Primary source: soldier's letter or diary excerpt from WWI
  • Primary source: excerpt from the Treaty of Versailles or a Mandate document
  • Historiographic excerpt: debate over the causes of interwar instability
  • Textbook: “War, Revolution, and the New Map” survey chapter
 Timeline project: chart the collapse of one interwar state's institutions and identify the turning points.
9
World War II and the Holocaust: A Crisis for the Ideology of Progress
  • Survivor or witness testimony excerpt (approved course text)
  • Primary source: an official Nazi state document (e.g., a discriminatory decree)
  • Historiographic excerpt: Hannah Arendt or comparable historian on bureaucracy and atrocity
  • Textbook: “The Second World War” survey chapter
 Analytical essay: did the machinery of the Holocaust discredit the ideology of progress, or did its architects simply claim to be advancing a progress narrative of their own? Defend a position.
10
Postwar Order: Cold War, Decolonization, and a New Middle East (1945–1970s)
  • Primary source: UN Partition Plan (1947) or Declaration of Independence excerpt
  • Primary source: a decolonization-era independence declaration from another postwar state
  • Historiographic excerpt: comparing models of postwar state formation
  • Textbook: “The Postwar World” survey chapter
 Comparative essay: place the founding of one 1945–70s state in the broader pattern of postwar decolonization.
11
The Contemporary World (1970s–Present)
  • Recent journal article or op-ed excerpt on a current regional conflict or diplomatic development
  • Primary source: a contemporary human-rights charter or treaty excerpt
  • Historiographic excerpt: a historian's reflection on whether “progress” remains a useful framework
  • Textbook: “The Contemporary World” survey chapter
 Capstone essay: revisit the course's central question — has the idea of progress held up across 600 years of this region's history? — drawing evidence from at least three units.
