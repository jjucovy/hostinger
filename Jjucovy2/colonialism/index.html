Colonialism

Empire and anti-colonial movements, 1880–1970.

Course description

The course examines the establishment of the major colonial empires throughout the world and the development of anti-colonial movements between 1880 and 1970, focusing on the British Raj, the Belgian Congo, the Middle East, and China.

Summer assignment

Read the following books:

  1. Forster, E. M. — A Passage to India (Penguin Classics, 2005)
  2. Munif, Abdelrahman — Cities of Salt (Vintage, 1989)

Be prepared on the first day of class to take a test on the content of these books, as well as Joseph Conrad's Heart of Darkness, which is assigned in your required North Shore Hebrew Academy English courses. A book review (5–7 pages) will be due within the first month of class, after these three books have been discussed during the first weeks of class.

Required books

  1. Hochschild, Adam — King Leopold's Ghost
  2. Preston, Diana — The Boxer Rebellion: The Dramatic Story of China's War on Foreigners that Shook the World in the Summer of 1900
  3. Gourevitch, Philip — We Wish to Inform You That Tomorrow We Will Be Killed With Our Families: Stories from Rwanda

Course syllabus

  1. Introduction
  2. The “New” Imperialism: the 19th-century division of the world
  3. Novels of Imperialism: Forster, Munif, and Conrad
  4. The British Raj
  5. Film: The Man Who Would Be King
  6. Leopold's Congo (reading King Leopold's Ghost)
  7. Responses and Resistance: Japan
  8. Responses and Resistance: China (reading The Boxer Rebellion)
  9. Responses and Resistance: Sudan
  10. Film: Khartoum
  11. Responses and Resistance: South Africa and the Boers
  12. Film: Breaker Morant
  13. The Final Surge: World War One and the Mid-East
  14. Film: Lawrence of Arabia
  15. The Japanese Assault and the Collapse of Asian Imperialism
  16. The Chinese Revolution
  17. The Indochina Revolution
  18. Freedom at Midnight: the Indian Subcontinent
  19. Israeli Independence
  20. Pan-Arabism
  21. Film: The Battle of Algiers
  22. The Imperial Aftermath — Failed States: Rwanda (reading We Wish to Inform You That Tomorrow We Will Be Killed With Our Families)

Tests

There will be two tests over the course of the semester.
