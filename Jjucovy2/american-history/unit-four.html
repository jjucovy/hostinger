Unit Four Synopsis

 Reconstruction (1865-77) Assignments 32-36

The Civil War, ended in defeat for the principle of states' rights and freedom for the slaves. But the role of the federal government, now a national government, still needed to be defined. More importantly, the fate of the freedmen was the central issue that faced (and still haunts) the United States.  Was the freedman to be allowed the equality that they actively demanded?

 The Gilded Age

The Gilded Age that followed the twin crises of the Civil War and Reconstruction was notable for the rapid economic growth of the United States, which became, by the end of the century, the world’s leading industrial and agricultural nation. The triumph of American industrialism was accompanied by rapid urbanization and population growth, including the arrival of vast numbers of immigrants from Southern and Eastern Europe, as well as from Mexico, Japan, and China. These developments — industrialization, commercialization, urbanization, and immigration — contributed to growing tensions between social classes, a relatively new feature of American life. Violent labor disputes led to local, state, and national government intervention, almost always on the side of management and capital, with the courts playing an especially important role in defending ownership interests through the growing use of injunctions. Farmers also experienced periodic distress and responded with a variety of protest movements, culminating in the Populist Revolt. Lastly, the Gilded Age witnessed the extension of American power and influence beyond the continent, with trade agreements soon followed by the acquisition of near and distant colonies, including those taken from Spain in the end-of-century Spanish-American War.
