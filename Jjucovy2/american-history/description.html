Course Description

This is a survey course in American History from the colonial period to the present. Aside from knowing the major factual details of American History, you are expected to demonstrate an ability to argue and write about important issues, themes, and debates concerning the history of the United States.

Everyone is expected to have the following reading materials:

America Past and Present by Robert Divine

Hypertext-Linked Resources

Assorted handouts in the course of the year

You are expected to complete each reading assignment by the day for which it is assigned even if you are absent. In the event of an absence, you are responsible for getting notes from a classmate. There will be occasional in-class writing and other writing assignments.

There will be periodic tests and quizzes (some unannounced) given during the term and a final exam at the end of each semester. Absence from these without a valid excuse will result in a failure. With a valid excuse you must make up the quiz or test within one week of your return to school.

Proper note taking is crucial- both for reading and in class. Develop a system which suits you. Do not bring the textbook (unless told to do so) to class-- bring your notes!

Aside from reading and analyzing the text, an important skill to work on will be the analysis of primary documents. Reading these primary sources critically is important to your success in this course and, just as importantly, it is important to the success of the course. There will be two research papers in the course this year. The requirements for the first paper will be given to you shortly.

I am available to meet with all of you during office hours which will be posted. Each of you must see me at least once each quarter to discuss any questions you have or for enrichment.
