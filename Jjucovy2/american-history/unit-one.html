Unit One Synopsis

Colonial Era (1607-1763) Assignments 1-3

The Spanish were the first Europeans to settle the New World. Over a hundred years later, England sent its first group of settlers to North America, beginning a wave of colonization that would eventually create the thirteen separate entities that comprised what we call colonial America. Colonial development was far from uniform. It was determined not only by the differing physical conditions along the coastline, but by the ideas and interest of those who came. Some sought economic advantage; some (very few in fact) came in pursuit of religious freedom. Some were brought against their will. Some colonies were property of the Crown, others were gifts to friends of the king or were business arrangements made by British trading companies. We will study the early colonial settlements because the organization and beliefs of those who created this short-lived English empire would profoundly affect the development of what became the United States.

The American Revolution (1763-1789)  Assignments 4-10

Colonial development was affected too, by the attitude of the English government toward the settlers. That attitude varied, dependent on the condition of the English economy and the tensions between England and the other inhabitants of the North American continent. The American revolution was the product of many forces-- political, philosophical, economic, and social. But for all its complexity, it created something which purported to be quite new; a model of a society based on the concepts of human equality and the right of the people to govern themselves. Whether or not the United States has lived up to that ideal, indeed, whether or not it is possible to create a democratic and egalitarian society, is part of the story we will examine during the rest of the year.
