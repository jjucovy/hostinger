Reconstruction

  • What were the reasons that freedom did not bring political, social or economic equality for the freedment?
    32.  Gettysburg Address
    The Gettysburg Address is like a story, a story about America.
    How does it resemble a story?
    Who does Lincoln include or not include in his story of America?
    What does Lincoln's story reveal about his plans for Reconstruction?
    33.  Reconstruction: Initial Plans
    a.  Read Divine pp. 455-460
    b.  Examine the opening section which gives a mini-biography of the career of Robert Smalls.  What is point that the author is trying to make by using this example?  Identify THREE major facts in this paragraph. Identify THREE major historical interpretations in this paragraph.  Finally, pay attention in the rest of the assignment (456-60) to the amount of text that focuses on the role of people like Smalls.  How many paragraphs develop the story of people like Smalls.
    c.  What were differences in the post-war goals and motives of Lincoln and the Congressional Republicans?
    d.  What were differences in the post-war goals and motives of Johnson and the Congressional Republicans?
    e.  Why did the relationship between Johnson and the Congressional Republicans start to deteriorate by Dec. 1865?
    34.  Radical Reconstruction
    a.  Read Divine pp. 460-464
    b.  The author asserts that the irreconcilable differences between Johnson and the Republicans was a "matter of principle".  What were the principles that divided them?  What were the motives (as explained by the author) of the Congressional Republicans?
    c.  What were most important EVENTS that led to the final break between Johnson and the Congressional Republicans?
    d.  Does the author assert that Congressional Reconstruction was radical?  Explain?
    e.  Was the Impeachment Crisis a victory or defeat for Congressional Republicans?
    35.  The Reconstruction South
    a.  Read Divine pp. 465-470
    b.  Why did the freedmen end up as sharecroppers instead of individual farm owners?
    c.  How did the freedmen react to the failure of the Union to adopt a land distribution program and to the imposition of Black Codes (as well as other forms of racial separation)?
    d.  The Southern Republican Party was made up of three social groups; what were these and what were their motives for joining and voting for the Republican Party?
    e.  How many paragraphs in the reading are devoted to the achievements or the Reconstruction governments?  How many paragraphs are devoted to their failures?  Are there any words used to describe the Reconstruction governments and Southern Republicans that are pejorative?
    36.  The Retreat from Reconstruction
    a.  Read Divine pp. 470-481
    b.  Between 1868 and 1876, the Republican Party and Federal government abandoned military Reconstruction and ultimately withdrew Federal troops from the South.  What, in order of importance, were the reasons for this shift?
    c.  Why would it correct to characterize the 14th and 15th Amendments as "moderate" instead of "radical"?
    d.  Which social class(es) benefitted the most from the victory of the Redeemers?  Which social class(es) benefited the least?
    e.  List three methods used by the Redeemers to gain and/or hold political power in the South?
    f.  Examine the photos and captions of the Klan (472) and the lynching (477).  Is there a "message" in the choices made by your textbook and by your teacher?  How many African-Americans were killed by terrorists between 1865 and 1877?
