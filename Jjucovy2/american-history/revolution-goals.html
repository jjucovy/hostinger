American Revolution Goals

  • How "revolutionary" was the American Revolution?
    4.  The North American Crisis
    a.  Read The Historians Debate.
    Which of the three schools regard the revolution as "revolutionary"?  If they agree that it was "revolutionary", how do they disagree about what made the Revolution "revolutionary"?
    b.  Read pages 134-145 and New England statistics.
    What do the statistics reveal about the New England economy on the eve of the Revolution?  Which "school" would be most likely to cite the statistics to support their interpretation of the Revolution? Why did colonists object to British policies between 1763 and 1774?  How would each "school" explain these colonial protests?
    5.  Declaring Independence
    a.  Read pages 145-148. and read the hypertext Jefferson Draft of the Declaration of Independence, making sure to use the hypertext to review changes to the final Declaration.
    What principles and ideas of John Locke did Jefferson adopt in the Declaration?  How would you explain the types of changes made to Jefferson's Draft of the Declaration of Independence? Does the Declaration AND the changes that were made support any of the "schools" in their interpretation of the Revolution?
    6.  War for Independence
    Read pages 148-158.
    What advantages and weaknesses did the combatants have in the American Revolution?  Do you think the colonies could have won the war without the assistance of foreign governments?   Does the textbook account of the war support a Conflict or Consensus view of the American History?
  • Was the Constitution a "Counter-Revolution"?
    7.  Limits of Revolutionary Change
    a.  Read Historians on the Constitution.  Which of the three schools would most likely agree with the Progressive School interpretation of the Revolution?  Which of the three schools conforms with a Consensus view of American History?
    b.  Read pages 161-168.?
    Did the victorious revolution bring about extensive, long-lasting political, social and economic changes?  Do the changes that did occur support any of the three "schools"?
    8.  The Articles of Confederation Era:  Experimenting with Self-Government
    a.  Read pages 168-177.
    "The Articles were too democratic for the planter and merchant elites." Defend or oppose with specific supporting information.
    9.  Restructured Republic
    a.  Read pages 177-185.
    Was the ratification of the Constitution an American Counter-Revolution?  What were the chief sources of conflict that obstructed the drafting of a new Constitution?
    10.  The Ratification Struggle
    a.  Read pages 185-189 and Federalist 10.pdf and Brutus' Reply
    What are the arguments made by Madison and Brutus regarding the size of the United States? Why is the issue of size so important for Madison to address? What would be the logical conclusion of Madison's argument regarding size? What assumptions about human nature underlay Madison's #10? Which "school" does the ratification struggle appear to support?
