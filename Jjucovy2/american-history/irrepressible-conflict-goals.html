The Irrepressible Conflict

  • Was the Civil War a "repressible" or "irrepressible" conflict?
    26.  Efforts at Compromise
    a.  Read Historian Debate
    b.  Read pages 391-397
    Why was the Wilmot Proviso, which never became law, such a symbol of the sectional crisis that was developing?
    Why did the Compromise of 1850 take so long to get through Congress? What parts of it dealt with issues that touched the moral consciousness of the North? How was it finally made into law?
    27.  Kansas-Nebraska and Political Realignment
    a.  Read pages 397-404
    What was Douglas's goal in proposing the Kansas-Nebraska Act? Did it serve to strengthen the forces of compromise or to undermine them?
    Why did the Republican Party emerge when it did? Was it a national or sectional party? What was its position on slavery? What group, or groups, of Americans did it best represent?
    28.  The House Divided
    a.  Read pages 404-415, Dred Scott v. Sanford
    What role did the Dred Scott decision play in destroying the chances that we could avoid war? What were its moral and political implications?
  • Why did the struggle to save the Union become a struggle to abolish slavery?
    29.  Secession and Civil War
    a.  Read Historians Debate
    b.  Read pages 423-429
    Could the United States have avoided war? At what points might that have been possible? When did war become inevitable? Were politicians to blame? Was slavery?
    30.  Civil War 1861-62
    Read pages 429-440, Emancipation Proclamation
    Given the circumstances in the Union and Confederacy, was a Northern victory a foregone conclusion? How could the South have won the war?
    31.  Civil War 1863-65
    Read pages 440-450, Gettysburg Address
    How would you assess Abraham Lincoln as 1) a military leader; 2) a political leader; 3) a moral leader? How did he use the issue of slavery to his political advantage? Was his abuse of the Bill of Rights justified?
