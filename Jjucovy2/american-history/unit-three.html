Unit Three Synopsis

The Impending Crisis (1848-1860) Assignments 26-28

Once the new national government was established and the Washington administration took office, political parties emerged quickly. The Federalists, led by Hamilton and Adams, believed in strong central government and mistrusted the states and "common" people. They felt the nation would endure only if its affairs were managed by an aristocracy of talent and believed, above all, that the future of the United States rested on its ability to demonstrate financial responsibility and strength. The Democratic-Republicans, led by Jefferson, were far more suspicious of centralized power. They had far more faith in the ordinary citizen's capacity to govern himself and extending the authority of the Federal government beyond what had been explicitly stated in the Constitution. The parties also disagreed about foreign affairs. As Europe was torn apart by the French Revolution and the Napoleonic Wars, Americans argued about their role in the world. Who would be our allies? Could we remain uninvolved when our economic health depended on trade with that war-torn Europe?  The victory of the Republicans in the election of 1800 in a hotly contested election that raised the specter of violence ended the domination of the Federalist Party.
During the Era of Good Feeling party conflict was temporarily dampened and American foreign policy took a direction that would continue to reverberate to the present day. The industrial revolution came to America, and with it the country experienced economic development that was eventually to help divide the United States.

 The Civil War (1860-1865) Assignments 29-31

With the end of the Era of Good feelings, the new democratic politics was dominated by Andrew Jackson and his enemy, Henry Clay. It was a time of economic expansion, growing nationalism, growing sectionalism and frenetic change.

Perhaps in response to the frenetic pace of American life, there emerged a host of new religions, religious revivals, and proposals for social reform to assist the disabled and the insane, prisoners and Alcohol drinkers. The Women's Movement was born in a small town in New York in 1848 and the Abolition movement emerged as a force that would threaten the society, economics and thinking of a large section of the country. It was a time when Utopian, communal societies were established, as people rejected the selfishness and materialism of America. It was the time when the Transcendentalists made their philosophical mark upon the nation.

American westward expansion was often the product of the slave question and it also brought the issue of slavery to the forefront of the national debate. As America added new states the battle was joined in Congress. Compromise was attempted, but the issue, both morally and economically, was increasingly not amenable to peaceable solutions. The battle over the extension of slavery led to yet another change in the American political system and, perhaps inexorably, to the opening shots of the Civil War.
