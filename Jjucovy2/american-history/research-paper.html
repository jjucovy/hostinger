American History Research Paper

The following is the schedule of assignments, with their due dates, for the research paper.  You are required to complete each step on time; each part will count toward your grade.  READ THIS ENTIRE DOCUMENT CAREFULLY.
Part Ia:  Topic Proposals – Wednesday September 23
Submit three proposed topics at the link that is provided.  The topic must be an event in American History from the Colonial Era up to 1992.  The topic should be relatively narrow, something that does not take up more than a paragraph in your American History textbook.  Broad topics such as  “Abraham Lincoln and Slavery”, “The Roaring Twenties”, “The Great Depression”, or “The McCarthy Era” are NOT acceptable.  Topics in cultural history should be avoided.  Examples of  acceptable topics would be “The War Hawks role in the declaration of war in the War of 1812”,  “Benjamin Franklin’s position regarding American Independence”
Part Ib:  Final Topic Proposal – Monday October19
Download the form provided.  Fill out the form for your research paper topic ONCE IT HAS BEEN APPROVED and submit it to Turnitin (if more than one of your topics has been approved, choose ONE of these for submission).
Part II Bibliography – Monday, October 26
Submit your proposed bibliography or research materials on Turnitin that properly documents each of the sources you are using for your research paper.  This must include at least five books AND three scholarly sources (e.g., articles).  A legitimate source is one with citations, meaning footnotes or endnotes.  For each of your proposed sources, your submission MUST ALSO include a photocopy of a single page from the source that contains a footnote, note, or parenthetical citation (some sources only list citations at the end of an article or chapter or book) OR a link to Amazon, Google Books or a similar online source in which the citations can be found.  When you actually write your paper, you must base your work on information from books and scholarly sources, not from encyclopedias or the Internet.  It is okay if you find new sources after you submit this bibliography, as long as you remember to add them.  We will discuss in class how to do a works cited page.  (The information is also in Citing Sources.)  You must start doing actual research and taking notes before this deadline even though your research will not be checked on this date
Part III  First Note Check Wednesday, November 18
As soon as possible, you should begin taking notes on the sources you are using for your paper.  These notes MUST be on index cards OR using note-taking software that mimics index cards.  Keep the index cards together with a rubber band, and submit them that way OR submit the computer note-taking file to me by email attachment.  It is okay to continue taking notes after this deadline, but you should be well on your way by this date.  We will discuss in class what you should be writing on the index cards.
Part IV  Second Note Check Friday, February 26
This is the same as the first check, except we are doing it again to make sure you are making progress.  By this time, you should be finishing up your research and moving on to organizing your paper.  Depending on how far along each person is for the first note check, not everyone will be required to submit notes for the second note check
Part V Thesis Statement Friday February 26
Submit a thesis paragraph.  The thesis paragraph should follow the guidelines for an opening paragraph
Part VI Outline Monday March 22
Submit a detailed outline of your paper.  The outline must be three typed pages (double-spaced) and should show what each paragraph in the paper is about, including the main idea of the paragraph and the supporting evidence for each point.  Be sure that you clearly state your thesis at the beginning of the outline.  You will receive instructions on how to do an outline
Part VII OPTIONAL Draft Friday April 16
You may submit a draft of your paper.  Only a complete draft will be accepted, no partial drafts.  These will be returned with comments in time for you to make revisions.  Handing in a draft is NOT a guarantee of a good grade.  Do not abuse this step with unreasonable expectations

Part VIII Final Draft Monday NIGHT 12 AM, May 10
Submit the final draft of your paper, 10-15 pages in length.  The paper must be submitted with the proper heading (to be discussed in class) and without a title page or any sort of cover.  Your paper must include a completed copy of your works cited page.  The pages should be numbered, including the works cited page, but there should be no number on the first page
