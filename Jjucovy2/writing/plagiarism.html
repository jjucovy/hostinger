Plagiarism & Academic Integrity

Plagiarism and Academic Integrity

North Shore Hebrew Academy History Department

 

 

Secondary Sources and Primary Sources

A good history paper must use quotes of or specific references to primary sources in order to support your thesis.  However, secondary sources are the most important resource that you will use in the writing of papers.   Secondary sources are important because high school students simply do not have access to archives that contain primary sources.  Therefore, primary source information can most efficiently be found in secondary sources.  In addition, secondary sources allow students to become acquainted with a variety of historical interpretations of specific historical events, enabling students to become more comfortable with developing their own historical interpretations.

 

What is Plagiarism?

  • Plagiarism includes the use of any word, phrase, sentence or idea that you borrow from another person without giving that person specific credit in writing.
  • Plagiarism is not limited to copying text directly from a book.  You are plagiarizing if you fail to cite a word, phrase, sentence or idea from any source, including: books, web pages, parents, tutors and friends.
  • If you are working with the help of others on your paper (tutors, parents, friends), your helpers should act as readers, not as writers.  It is acceptable for them to point out mistakes or problem areas, but it is not acceptable for your helpers to correct them.

 

How to Avoid Plagiarism

One of the easiest ways to avoid plagiarism is to take careful and thorough notes.  You should use quotation marks when you are copying exact words in your notes, and you should write down exactly where your information is coming from (author, title, page number).  In addition:

  • Make sure that you include a Works Cited page on all critical essays—even if the only book included is the text on which your paper is written.  You should consult Citing Sources (available online at the English Department website) for information about how to format a Works Cited page.
  • If you receive the assistance of a tutor, you are required to let your teacher know that you have done so. 
  • Ask your teacher if you have any questions about what may constitute plagiarism.
  • Visit the North Shore Hebrew Academy Writing Clinic for answers to questions about plagiarism and proper citation. 

 

What Happens if You Plagiarize

Because the North Shore Hebrew Academy community believes personal integrity is integral to academic achievement and views dishonesty in any form as reprehensible in terms of religious as well as secular ideals, the consequences of plagiarism will be severe.  Consistent with school policy, the consequences for a first infraction will most likely include all of the following:

  • A grade of F or zero on the assignment
  • The requirement to rewrite it (without an opportunity for a new grade)
  • A substantial impact on your class grade for the semester
  • A conference between your parents and a school administrator
  • A copy of the plagiarized assignment kept in your permanent North Shore Hebrew Academy file—the inclusion of which will have an impact on faculty and administrative decisions regarding college recommendations, senior awards and admission to AP or Honors classes in your senior year

 

The consequences of a second infraction will include all of the above, with the potential addition of:

  • Suspension
  • Academic/social probation
  • Expulsion
  • Legal prosecution

 

Note: Plagiarism is considered just as severe an offense no matter the size or import of the assignment—whether it is a heavily weighted semester critical essay or an ungraded portfolio assignment.
