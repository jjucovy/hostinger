Topic Summary

Background Reading and Summary
The summary step is extremely important and should not be taken lightly.  If you have a good overview of your topic-- such as where it fits into the broader history of the place or time period, important events connected with the topic, important dates and names connected with the topic-- you will have a lot easier time consulting your research material/books and taking notes.
Has a teacher ever told you NOT to use an encyclopedia?   That SHOULD BE the FIRST book you consult to learn more about your topic.  Do some background reading (such as an encyclopedia) to get an idea about the event.  Wikipedia is a superb source to get an overview of the subject.  Another place to read about your topic is your history textbook, since it should probably cover your topic in a paragraph or page that also places the topic in a larger context.  Lastly, for modern European history, the Rise of Modern Europe, and, for the United States, the New American Nation series are excellent sources for learning quickly about your topic.  The North Shore Hebrew Academy library has many of the books in both these series.
When you consult a few of these sources, you should TAKE NOTES as you go along, as this will save you a great deal of time and effort later on as you prepare to write your paper.  Once you have consulted enough sources, you should write a summary of the topic (the length will depend on the grade that you are in).  You may obtain this information from any source, and you do not need to use the proper format for citations.  All you need to do is indicate where you got the information from at the end of the summary.  As with all of the work you do, you should also keep a detailed record of which sources you have used and, where applicable, the page numbers of where you found information.  The assignment must be typed, using standard format (double spaced, 12 point Times New Roman font, default margins)
