The Agrarian Myth in Gilded Age America

The years between 1866 and 1901 in American History are often referred to as the Gilded Age. These twenty-five years witnessed a rapid growth of population, the greatest wave of immigration to the nation's shores, tremendous growth in industry, and increasing urbanization with the attendant problems of poverty, slums, and disease. In this reading, we examine the fate of the Agrarian Myth — the Jeffersonian ideal of an agricultural republic — during the Gilded Age, beginning with historian Frederick Jackson Turner's influential 1893 essay.

Frederick Jackson Turner, "The Significance of the Frontier in American History" (1893)

In a recent bulletin of the Superintendent of the Census for 1890 appear these significant words: "Up to and including 1880 the country had a frontier of settlement, but at present the unsettled area has been so broken into by isolated bodies of settlement that there can hardly be said to be a frontier line." This brief official statement marks the closing of a great historic movement. Up to our own day American history has been in a large degree the history of the colonization of the Great West. The existence of an area of free land, its continuous recession, and the advance of American settlement westward, explain American development.

American social development has been continually beginning over again on the frontier. This perennial rebirth, this fluidity of American life, this expansion westward with its new opportunities, furnish the forces dominating American character. The true point of view in the history of this nation is not the Atlantic coast, it is the Great West.

Within a decade, four marked changes have occurred in our national development that, taken together, constitute a revolution. First, the exhaustion of the supply of free land and the closing of the movement of Western advance as an effective factor in American development. Second, a concentration of capital in the control of fundamental industries as to make a new epoch in the economic development of the United States — the iron, the coal, and the railroads of the country falling under the domination of a few great corporations. Third, the expansion of the United States politically and commercially into lands beyond the seas. And fourth, the political parties of the United States now tend to divide on issues that involve the question of Socialism, as seen in the rise of the Populist party.

Whatever shall be the outcome of the rush of this huge industrial modern United States to its place among the nations of the earth, the formation of its Western democracy will always remain one of the wonderful chapters in the history of the human race. She gave to the world such types as the farmer Thomas Jefferson, with his Declaration of Independence, his statute for religious toleration, and his purchase of Louisiana. She gave us Andrew Jackson, who broke down the traditions of conservative rule and opened the doors of government to the populace. She gave us Abraham Lincoln, whose grasp of the axe-handle of the pioneer was no firmer than his grasp of the helm of the ship of state as it made its way through the storm of civil war.

The paths of the pioneer have widened into broad highways. The forest clearing has expanded into affluent commonwealths. Let us see to it that the ideals of the pioneer in his log cabin shall enlarge into the spiritual life of a democracy where civic power shall dominate and utilize individual achievement for the common good.

Questions to consider

  1. Why does Turner believe that the United States is witnessing the close of one era and the beginning of another?
  2. What specific features of the new era distinguish it from the one that preceded it?
  3. What contributions did the frontier make to the shaping of America, according to Turner?
  4. What dangers and challenges to American values and behavior resulted from the "closing of the frontier"?
  5. Are the characteristics developed on the frontier, in Turner's view, still relevant to American society once the frontier is gone?
