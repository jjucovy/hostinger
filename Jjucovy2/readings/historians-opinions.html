Historians

Learning the opinions of historians is a critical part of research for and writing a history research paper.

  • You become familiar with the significance of your topic

  • You learn about areas of your topic that professional historians disagree about

  • You start to narrow down your topic to an area that historians disagree about OR to an area that historians appear to have paid little attention to

  • You begin to ask your own questions and form your own answers on the topic as a first step toward developing a thesis

How to discover what historians think

  • Read the preface or introduction in the books that you have acquired

  • Look for articles about historians' opinions on JSTOR.  Use search terms such as "War of 1812 Historians Debates" (you may need to insert quote marks around key phrases and use logical search terms such as "and")

  • Look for books (the North Shore Hebrew Academy Library has many of these books) containing articles by a number of historians about a single topic (the Heath Series for example)

How to use historians opinions in your paper

  • The thesis paragraph should give a brief summary of how historians view the topic

  • There should be a separate section of your paper that describes what historians think about your topic in greater detail, with some quotes of the historians' own words.  This section appears after the section of your paper that summarizes the topic
