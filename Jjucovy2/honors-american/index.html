Honors American History

America Since 1960

Course description

In 1960 the United States of America was the richest and most powerful polity that the world had ever seen. The foundation myth of America retained a powerful hold on its population, not to mention the rest of the world. Over the course of the next four decades that myth would be challenged by the Rights Revolution (beginning with the Civil Rights movement), the rise of a distinctive youth culture, the de-industrialization of America, and a series of disastrous developments in foreign affairs, such as the Vietnam War, the "terrorist" challenge, and the rise of alternative power bases in the rest of the world even as the Cold War was "won."

The course will focus on the "Culture Wars" or, as Isserman and Kazin entitle it, the Civil War, that erupted in the United States as the foundation myth of America began to dissolve — a struggle between those who sought to redefine that myth, to make it "relevant" once again in the face of the vast transformations within the United States, and those who defended the national story, regarding any alterations as heresy and treason.

Required books

  • Falling Man — Don DeLillo (Scribner)
  • Catch-22 — Joseph Heller
  • America Divided: The Civil War of the 1960s — Maurice Isserman and Michael Kazin (Oxford University Press)
  • The Crying of Lot 49 — Thomas Pynchon
  • The Age of Reagan: A History, 1974–2008 — Sean Wilentz (Harper)
