Germany 1920–45

The collapse of Weimar, the Nazi rise to power, and the Second World War.

Course description

German History from the early days of the Weimar Republic to the end of the Second World War. The course will survey the collapse of the republic, the Nazi rise to power, the Nazification of German society, the persecution of the Jews (origins and implementation of the Final Solution), and the Second World War.

Required books

  • Spielvogel, Jackson — Hitler and Nazi Germany: A History
  • Browning, Christopher — Ordinary Men: Reserve Police Battalion 101 and the Final Solution in Poland

Tests

There will be two tests: one in late February and one on the final day of class in March.

Primary source paper

Length: 3–4 pages.

Choose a single primary source either from or about the Nazi period, broadly defined as 1919–45. This can be, for example, a speech, law, letter, diary entry, photograph, film, or poster. Write a 3–4 page analysis of the source. When writing a small source analysis, one examines its context and what it reveals about the time period, the location, the society and, perhaps, the individual who created it. One is attentive not simply to the content, but to the audience, if any, that the source was addressed to, and the purpose, if any, behind the creation of the source. Bear in mind, the source does not have to come from the Nazi period — a perfectly acceptable paper could be written based on a film made in Hollywood about the Nazi period, although such a source requires a high degree of sophistication in film studies to analyze well.
